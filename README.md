# Dennis Gerrits: Your City Companion

Comprehensive Lovable Prompt for Dennis Gerrits

Project Name: Dennis Gerrits — Personal Travel Companion & Storyteller

Project Goal:

Build a premium, calm, and deeply personal portfolio and booking website for Dennis Gerrits. Dennis is not a traditional tour guide; he is a personal travel companion and storyteller who helps culturally curious travellers (primarily American, 55+) feel a city rather than just see it. The website must immediately convey trust, calmness, and authenticity. It should feel like a conversation with a knowledgeable friend, completely devoid of commercial pressure, visual noise, or typical tourism clichés.

1. Brand Identity & Visual Design (Midnight Heritage Concept)

The visual direction is based on the "Midnight Heritage" concept — dramatic, refined, and intimate, appealing to travellers seeking deeper stories and a sophisticated, unhurried experience.

Color Palette:

•Primary Background: Warm off-white/cream (#F5F1E8) to create breathing room and calmness.

•Text & High Contrast: Near black (#1A1A1A) for excellent readability.

•Primary Accent: Deep purple (#2D0036) for a sense of mystery and depth.

•Secondary Accent: Bordeaux/Burgundy (#7B1828) for warmth and heritage.

•Highlight: Muted Gold (#D4AF37) used very sparingly for subtle elegance.

Typography:

•Headings: Bebas Neue. Use it strong, large, and with slight letter-spacing to create a grounded, statement feel.

•Body Text: Outfit. Clean, subtly elegant, and highly readable for longer storytelling paragraphs.

Logo & Identity Element:

•Do not use a traditional corporate logo. Instead, use a personal introduction approach: "Hello, I am Dennis."

•Incorporate a subtle, hand-drawn portrait illustration of Dennis (green #59A574 or adapted to the dark palette) as a human anchor, not a dominant brand mark. It should feel like a signature.

Visual Vibe & UI Principles:

•Space & Calm: Generous white space (padding/margins). Elements should never feel cramped.

•No Pressure: Avoid aggressive "Book Now" buttons, pop-ups, or urgent animations.

•Authenticity: Use high-quality, candid photography (documentary style, no staged stock photos).

•Layout: Editorial and magazine-like, rather than a standard SaaS or e-commerce grid.

2. Tone of Voice & Copywriting

•Core Sentence: "A personal, trust-based way of experiencing places — guided by someone who feels like a friend."

•Tone: Calm, personal, direct, honest, and spacious. It should read like a thoughtful conversation.

•Nuance: Unhurried (not slow), open (not overly intimate), welcoming (not overly emotional).

•Avoid: Commercial jargon, pushy sales copy, overly poetic fluff, or corporate stiffness. Use "I" instead of "we".

3. Page Structure & Features (Phase 1 Core)

The website should be structured as a seamless, multi-page experience (or a very well-segmented long-scroll with distinct pages).

1. Homepage (The Introduction)

•Hero Section: A calm, spacious introduction. "Hello, I'm Dennis. I don't show a city — I translate it." Include the hand-drawn portrait.

•Philosophy: A brief section explaining the "walking alongside, not guiding from the front" approach.

•The Experience: Highlighting depth over highlights, connection without pressure.

2. About Dennis (The Storyteller)

•A deeper dive into his background, his passion for history and culture, and why he does this.

•Focus on building trust and showing his grounded, thoughtful presence.

3. A Day in the Life (The Approach)

•Explain that there are no fixed tours. Instead, it is a shared experience shaped around the guest.

•Describe the rhythm of a day: slow-paced, adaptable, stopping for coffee, discussing architecture or local life.

4. Build Your Day (Interests & Passions)

•An interactive but calm section where guests can see the themes Dennis is passionate about (e.g., Hidden History, Local Food Culture, Architecture, Street Art).

•This replaces standard "Tour Packages" with "Shared Interests."

5. Get in Touch (Video Call or Callback)

•The primary call-to-action.

•Options: "Schedule a short introductory video call" (Calendly integration) or "Request a callback."

•The framing must be low-threshold: "Let's see if we are a good match for your trip."

6. FAQ & Practicalities

•Clear, honest answers about pacing, group sizes (private only), pricing philosophy, and accessibility for 55+ travellers.

7. For Travel Agents (B2B)

•A dedicated, clean sub-page for concierges and travel designers detailing collaboration terms and direct contact.

4. Technical & Functional Requirements

•Responsive Design: Must be flawless on tablet and mobile, as older travellers often use iPads. Font sizes must remain legible.

•Performance: Fast loading, no heavy blocking scripts.

•Interactions: Keep animations to an absolute minimum. Perhaps a gentle fade-in on scroll, but nothing that distracts or creates "visual noise."

•Forms: Clean, simple contact forms with large input fields and clear labels.

•Navigation: A simple, sticky (but unobtrusive) header or a clean hamburger menu that doesn't hide essential information.

5. First Impression Goal (The 10-Second Test)

When the generated site loads, the immediate feeling must be:

1."This is different from standard tour websites."

2."This feels incredibly calm and spacious."

3."I trust this person."

4."I would like to meet him."

Please generate the complete frontend (React/Tailwind/Vite) adhering strictly to these brand guidelines, the Midnight Heritage color palette, and the specified typography. Ensure the layout feels editorial, human, and completely unhurried.
